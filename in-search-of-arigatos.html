FIELD NOTE · TOKYO

In Search of Arigatos..

Day 2, Trip to College

Initial days to college were always a little tidy and sharp. Remembering which lifts to take, which route to follow, which stairs to find — seemed difficult. Like a detective, I had to remember the signs. Even the signages initially looked like Egyptian walls. I started memorising anime figures I see along. Lifts were also no different; so many of them to remember.

One random day, as usual, happy to have found the right lift, I stepped in.

As we reached our floor, the person standing by the panel held the door open while everyone filed out. One by one, they stepped past him, each person gave a small bow and a soft “arigato gozaimashita”.

I also stepped out, without a sign or gratitude.

Rude me.

Actually, I didn’t know arigato(Japanese way of “Thank You”) also. Pardon my arrogance.

Second day also, the same happened.

Same spot.

Someone to press the button, same bow and showering arigatos.

Then I noticed, the guy was no random, he mostly enters at last, and he kept holding the button till all the oldies had filed out.

I did some ChatGPT. It turns out that I wasn’t watching mere politeness. It was some sort of junior duty. There is an entire unwritten public space system running quietly —Kamiza-shimoza—literally “upper seat, lower seat”. Back row is occupied by kamiza, the older lot, while the younger generation will be the one near the elevator buttons.

Nobody assigns this job.

Nobody explains it.

No-one insists.

This is not just followed in Lifts; similar idea is followed in taxis, meeting rooms, seating arrangements etc. No complaints if someone doesn’t follow. The nice one seems to know and abide by it.

Here, some lift also has some small folded seat or platforms at the back. No, it is not for the oldies to sit while travelling. It is for them when they are stuck due to some emergencies. A country with tens of thousands of elevators and frequent earthquake warnings had everything prepared.

They really have thought of everything.

Next week learning all about the theory of being the lift operator, I was all ready to get some bows and arigato gozaimashita.

Yeah, it is slightly difficult to get some attention from them otherwise.

These days my biggest challenge is finding a lift where I'm still the youngest by the panel — I clearly showed up in this country a few years too late. Now I get to "operate" maybe once a week, like a benched player brought in for a nostalgia run.

Vishnu Chandran
